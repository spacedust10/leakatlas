## 5. Limitations

- **Scale.** Two small datasets (Adult, Fashion-MNIST), two training-set sizes each, 3 seeds, and 7, 3 OUT reference models per record (Adult, Fashion-MNIST). LiRA-style studies use up to 256 shadow models; per-record scores here are noisier.
- **Attack coverage.** ML Privacy Meter implements RMIA and LOSS only (no LiRA); every defense is audited with one strong reference-model attack. A stronger or adaptive attacker could find more.
- **Threshold transfer.** LOSS thresholds calibrated on references reach a realized FPR of up to 6.8% against a 1% target; RMIA's per-example calibration transfers. Realized FPR is reported next to every shadow-calibrated TPR.
- **Weak labels under defenses.** With DP-SGD and MIAShield the attack flags members at roughly the FPR rate, so the 'vulnerable' label used to score the proxies is mostly attack noise there.
- **Small subgroups.** The smallest Adult race groups have Other n=79, Amer-Indian-Eskimo n=86 training records at the larger size; their intervals are wide.
- **Defense implementations.** MIAShield uses only the exact-signature exclusion oracle (ESE), re-implemented in PyTorch from the authors' TensorFlow notebook. DP-SGD hyper-parameters were chosen on a defender validation split, not tuned per ε.
- **Generalization.** Small MLP/CNN models trained from scratch; results may not transfer to large or pre-trained models, other data modalities, or skewed membership priors.
